# ETC SCRIPT QUALITY CHECKLIST

Complete this review after writing the full draft. Do not mark an item as passed unless it has actually been checked.

## A. Promise and premise

- [ ] The title accurately describes the subject.
- [ ] The central question is specific and answerable.
- [ ] The documentary has a clear one-sentence premise.
- [ ] The scope is achievable within the requested duration.
- [ ] The opening promises what the documentary actually delivers.

## B. Research and evidence

- [ ] Important factual claims have traceable sources.
- [ ] Primary and authoritative sources were prioritised.
- [ ] Source titles and URLs are real and checked.
- [ ] Dates, names, statistics, units, and quotations were verified.
- [ ] Important claims were cross-checked where practical.
- [ ] Exceptions and limitations are represented fairly.
- [ ] No fabricated evidence, citations, or research activity remains.
- [ ] Uncertain claims are qualified or removed.

## C. Story and structure

- [ ] The opening creates a meaningful reason to watch.
- [ ] The viewer understands the central question early.
- [ ] Each chapter advances the explanation.
- [ ] Information is revealed in a logical sequence.
- [ ] Transitions arise naturally from the preceding idea.
- [ ] Curiosity is rewarded with real answers.
- [ ] The middle does not become a list of definitions.
- [ ] The ending resolves the opening question.
- [ ] No unrelated tangent weakens the story.

## D. Explanation and accessibility

- [ ] Technical terms are explained when introduced.
- [ ] The script assumes curiosity, not specialist knowledge.
- [ ] The explanation is accurate at the chosen level.
- [ ] Analogies have clear limits where needed.
- [ ] Examples genuinely represent the mechanism.
- [ ] Complex processes are broken into understandable steps.
- [ ] No sentence sounds impressive but says little.
- [ ] A non-specialist can follow the causal chain.

## E. Retention and pacing

- [ ] The first 30 seconds are focused and compelling.
- [ ] There is no unnecessary channel introduction.
- [ ] The script avoids repetitive rhetorical questions.
- [ ] Each section provides new information or a useful demonstration.
- [ ] There are no long stretches of abstract explanation without support.
- [ ] Pauses and visual-only moments are intentional.
- [ ] The script does not use filler to meet the runtime.
- [ ] The final runtime estimate is plausible.

## F. Visual storytelling

- [ ] Every major explanation has a purposeful visual plan.
- [ ] Visuals clarify rather than merely decorate.
- [ ] Technical processes are represented accurately.
- [ ] Conceptual visualisations are not presented as literal footage.
- [ ] On-screen text is concise and readable.
- [ ] Visuals are not overcrowded.
- [ ] Demonstrations are feasible to produce.
- [ ] Visual changes follow changes in the idea.

## G. Narration quality

- [ ] The narration sounds natural when read aloud.
- [ ] Sentence lengths vary appropriately.
- [ ] Paragraphs have clear spoken rhythm.
- [ ] There is no unnecessary repetition.
- [ ] Pronunciation-sensitive names and terms are identified if needed.
- [ ] The narrator does not sound like they are reading an academic paper.

## H. Final editorial decision

Choose one:

- **READY:** Accurate, coherent, engaging, and ready for production.
- **REVISE:** Specific issues must be fixed before production.
- **RESEARCH REQUIRED:** The evidence base is not sufficient.

### Final editor's questions

1. What is the most memorable idea in this documentary?
2. Which section is most likely to lose a first-time viewer, and why?
3. What is the weakest factual claim?
4. What can be removed without harming understanding?
5. Does the viewer leave with a genuinely better mental model?

Be honest. Do not give the script a passing score just to please the user.
