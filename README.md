# ETC — Exploring Technology & Computing

> An independent educational video project exploring the ideas, people, inventions, and systems that shaped the world of technology.

## Our vision

ETC is being developed as an original YouTube channel about **information technology, computer science, programming, and the history of technological invention**.

The goal is not merely to teach people which buttons to press or how to copy code. We want to help viewers understand:

- How an idea becomes an invention.
- Why a technology was created and what problem it solved.
- How programming languages and computer systems work.
- Who the people behind important discoveries were.
- How today's digital world developed over time.
- What is happening beneath the surface of the tools we use every day.

Technology should feel understandable, interesting, and worth being curious about—even when the subject is complex.

## What we want to explore

Our videos can cover several connected areas:

### 1. Information technology and computer science
- How computers work, from hardware to software.
- Operating systems, networks, databases, the internet, and cybersecurity.
- Algorithms, data structures, and the foundations of computing.
- How everyday digital services and applications work.

### 2. Programming languages and code
- The origins and evolution of languages such as C, C++, Java, Python, JavaScript, and others.
- Why different languages were created and what makes them different.
- How code is translated and executed by a computer.
- Programming concepts explained through examples, experiments, and demonstrations.
- The people and ideas behind programming language design.

### 3. Inventions and breakthroughs
- Important inventions in computing and information technology.
- The problems, experiments, failures, and breakthroughs behind them.
- How technologies changed industries and everyday life.
- The relationship between scientific research and practical engineering.

### 4. The history of IT
- Early calculating machines and the origins of modern computers.
- The development of transistors, integrated circuits, and microprocessors.
- The birth of the internet and the World Wide Web.
- The evolution of personal computers, mobile devices, and software.
- Important companies, researchers, engineers, and historical moments.

### 5. Modern and emerging technology
- Artificial intelligence and machine learning.
- Open-source software and developer communities.
- Cloud computing, robotics, and new computing architectures.
- New technologies, their real capabilities, and their limitations.

These are areas of exploration, not a fixed list. Individual videos should have a clear question or central idea.

## Our storytelling approach

A reference point for our production is the documentary-style script about quantum physics that is stored in this repository. Its value to us is the way a difficult scientific subject can be approached through a narrative.

We want to learn from that approach while developing our own voice and structure.

A typical episode may follow this progression:

1. **The question or mystery** — Begin with something surprising, useful, or difficult to explain.
2. **The context** — Establish the historical, scientific, or practical situation.
3. **The problem** — Explain what people could not do, understand, or solve at the time.
4. **The people and ideas** — Introduce the relevant thinkers, engineers, inventors, and competing ideas.
5. **The mechanism** — Explain how the technology or concept actually works.
6. **The demonstration** — Use code, diagrams, experiments, visualisations, or real examples wherever possible.
7. **The consequences** — Show how the invention or idea changed technology and society.
8. **The open question** — End with what remains unresolved, what changed next, or what viewers should think about.

Not every episode needs every step. The structure should serve the subject rather than become a formula.

## Editorial principles

- **Accuracy before drama.** Interesting storytelling must not distort technical or historical facts.
- **Explain, don't merely define.** Show mechanisms, causes, trade-offs, and consequences.
- **Make complexity accessible without making it false.** Avoid oversimplifications that create misconceptions.
- **Use evidence.** Prefer primary sources, technical documentation, research papers, books, credible archives, and reliable reporting.
- **Separate fact from interpretation.** Make uncertainty, disagreement, and historical gaps clear.
- **Demonstrate where possible.** Use working code, experiments, diagrams, and visual examples to make abstract ideas concrete.
- **Respect the people and history.** Give appropriate context and credit; avoid reducing complicated discoveries to a single heroic story.
- **Create original work.** Research can inspire an episode, but scripts, narration, explanations, visuals, and edits should be our own. Do not copy another creator's wording or structure line by line.

## Production workflow

A video should move through a deliberate process:

1. Choose a focused topic and formulate the central question.
2. Research the subject and collect trustworthy sources.
3. Build a fact-checked outline and timeline, where relevant.
4. Write an original narration script.
5. Review technical explanations, names, dates, and claims.
6. Plan visuals: screen recordings, code demonstrations, diagrams, archival material, and original graphics.
7. Record narration and produce the video.
8. Review the final edit for clarity, accuracy, pacing, audio, and attribution.
9. Publish with a useful description, sources, and references.

## The standard we are aiming for

Every video should leave the viewer with more than a collection of facts. It should give them a clearer mental model of a technology, a better understanding of how an idea developed, and a reason to keep asking questions.

**ETC is about understanding the technology behind the modern world—how it began, how it works, and where it may lead.**
